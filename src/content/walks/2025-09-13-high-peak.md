## The day

September 2025. 14.9 miles round with 1011m of climbing, 5h56 on the move.

A bit of light drizzle but nothing that lasted, 8 to 12°C, wind gusting to 19mph.

## Walking with

Just me and Ember.

## The route

The biggest climbing day in the collection: 1011m, and the only one over a
thousand.

Up from the Derwent valley and north west onto the Alport and Bleaklow
moors, topping out at 506m around SK 128 932. Out to the Snake Road and back
round, dropping to 220m before the return.

The second fifth carries 337m of the ascent, which is the pull onto the
plateau. There is then a genuine sting in the tail: 194m in the last fifth,
after four hours of moor.

This is remote ground. Bleaklow and Alport Moor are the sort of place where
the path disappears into peat and the next landmark is a long way off. Worth
a map and the confidence to use it.

## For your four legged companion

Access land, so the 1 March to 31 July lead rule applies, and there is stock
on the lower ground.

Water on the moor is peaty and slow. The valley has plenty. In between, not
much.

Peat groughs, tussock and bog. This is the hardest going of any walk here for
a dog, and the least forgiving if anything goes wrong.

## Parking and practicalities

Fairholmes below the Derwent dam. Visitor centre, toilets and a cafe, and the
257 bus from Sheffield.

No shelter once you are on the moor, and no easy way off it. Treat this one as
a proper day out.
