## The day

September 2025. 14.9 miles round with 1011m of climbing, 5h56 on the move.

A bit of light drizzle but nothing that lasted, 8 to 12°C, wind gusting to 19mph.

## Walking with

Just me and Ember.

## The route

The biggest climbing day in the collection. 1011m, and the only one here over
a thousand.

Up from the Derwent valley and north west onto Alport Moor, topping out at
506m around SK 128 932, then out towards the Snake Road and back round. The
low point is 220m on the return.

The reason to come this way is Alport Castles, off to the west above Alport
Dale. At over half a mile long it is reckoned to be the largest landslip in
Britain. What happened is that weak Carboniferous shales gave way beneath the
harder Millstone Grit on top, and the grit came down in blocks. The biggest
of them, The Tower, stands isolated on the valley side and from a distance
looks uncannily like a motte and bailey castle. Hence the name.

You do not have to go down to them. The view from the moor above is the one
worth having.

Below in the dale is Alport Castles Farm, where a Love Feast has been held in
the barn for at least 250 years. John Wesley preached there in the 18th
century, and every July the congregation still share fruitcake and water from
a two-handled loving cup.

The climbing is front-loaded and then stings: 337m in the second fifth
getting onto the plateau, then 194m in the last after four hours of moor.

This is remote ground. On Alport Moor and Bleaklow the path disappears into
peat and the next landmark is a long way off. Worth a map and the confidence
to use it.

## For your four legged companion

Access land, so the 1 March to 31 July lead rule applies whether or not there
is stock, and there are sheep on the lower ground.

Water on the moor is peaty and slow moving. The valley has plenty. In between,
not much.

Peat groughs, tussock and bog. This is the hardest going of any walk here for
a dog and the least forgiving if something goes wrong. The ground above the
Castles is broken and steep, so keep your dog close there.

## Parking and practicalities

Fairholmes below the Derwent dam. Visitor centre, toilets and a cafe, and the
257 bus from Sheffield along the Snake Road terminates there.

No shelter once you are on the moor and no quick way off it. Treat this as a
proper day out.
